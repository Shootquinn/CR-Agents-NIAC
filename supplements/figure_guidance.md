# Figure guidance

Follow all 12 rules.

1. Plan 3 to 7 numbered figures for the 3 body pages.
2. Give figures 10 to 30 percent of the body text area.
3. Cover three roles in this order: mission context early (page 2), mechanism in the middle, feasibility evidence last (test photo, existing-product photo, published method, or cost comparison).
4. Mix types: at least one concept render or CAD view, and at least one real-world photo or literature schematic.
5. Reuse the overview graphic as Figure 1 when it shows the mission scene.
6. Make most figures 30 to 60 percent of the text width, wrapped by text. Use full width only for wide subjects.
7. Start each caption with "Figure N:" and use that one form everywhere. Write 1 to 3 sentences, 6 to 45 words. Sentence 1 names what is shown. Later sentences give a dimension, an omission, or the source.
8. Put the physical size in the caption or as a dimension callout on the figure.
9. Cite the source in the caption, in the same (Author, Year) form as the body, for each reproduced or adapted figure.
10. Cite every figure by number in the body text.
11. Put the color key in the caption or a legend on the figure when color carries meaning. Use at most one arrow or label per photo.
12. Write one claim per figure ("this figure shows that ___"). Never join two figures in one image without a visible divider and a label per panel. Keep tables as text.

## Image prompt line

A figure that code cannot draw carries `Image prompt: <laconic prompt>` on the line after its caption. A code-drawn plot has no such line. The user makes the image with any tool they choose and drops the file in figures/.

## Worked example

Figure 1: A blimp in Jupiter's atmosphere. The envelope is 40 m long.
Image prompt: A silver research blimp drifting through banded tan and white Jupiter cloud tops, realistic hardware, no text, landscape 16:9.
