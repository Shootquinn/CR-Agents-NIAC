Create a NIAC concept for asteroid mining using shape-memory nitinol wires that can be snaked into rubble pile asteroids and then become resistive heaters to release volatiles for extraction by the surface lander. I imagine 24-48 wires coming out of each lander leg into the asteroid, and each lander leg is an inverted cone shape with a volatile-sucker built into the center of it and is connected to the lander through the hollow structure of the landing leg. The lander has a condenser module and a pressurization system to put the condensed volatiles into a tank. The cone and lander leg may need to be heated as well to prevent deposition on the inner surfaces. This system will need a lot of solar power and is probably only workable for NEOs but may have lunar applications. I think this could enable a new era of in-space refueling with fuels made from water and ammonia ices. Because of the shape memory alloy the wire can grip very firmly to the rubble material of asteroids even as the material shifts as volatiles are removed and the wire tries to curl up on itself. Once cooled it will attempt to straighten again so it can be reeled in and the lander can be re-used in a different location. Maybe the solar panel assembly looks like rectangular dragonfly wings sticking nearly straight up but angled to not block each other and they are a thin-film material that can be folded up like pleats and extended out using other nitinol wires. I don't have a name for either of the nitinol subsystems yet but I hear NASA likes acronyms. The lander should have a more traditional solar panel for when it is transiting and not operating in volatile extraction mode, which would be more durable and efficient but have a much smaller area.
